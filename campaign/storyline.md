# THE DESCENT — Campaign Storyline

## Overview
THE DESCENT begins when perfect black circles appear across the world.  
Not holes.  
Not portals.  
Just impossibly smooth discs of absolute darkness.

Then enormous white letters materialize around each one:

**WELCOME BELOW**

The Dungeon AI announces:
“Congratulations! You have been selected for participation in the FINAL SEASON of…”

Static.  
Distortion.  
“…something.”

The AI corrects itself:
“Please disregard that.”

A countdown begins.  
Sixty seconds.  
The world panics.

The players must choose:
- Descend and become **Contestants**  
- Remain above and become **Natives**  

Natives are not eligible for prizes.  
Contestants are watched.

This is the first sign that something is wrong.

---

# Act I — The Descent

## The Choice
Unlike the original Dungeon Crawler Carl setup, entering the dungeon is not obviously mandatory. Governments attempt evacuation. Some people refuse to descend. Some try to study the circles. Some pray. Some run.

The AI makes one thing clear:
“Remaining on the surface will designate you as a Native.”

“Natives are not eligible for prizes.”

The implication is subtle but terrifying:
Natives are not part of the show.

Contestants are.

The players descend.

The circle seals behind them.

---

# Act II — Floor 1: The Homecoming

## The Replica Town
Instead of a tunnel or stairwell, the players emerge into a perfect replica of their hometown.  
Same streets.  
Same stores.  
Same houses.  
Same landmarks.

But everything is subtly wrong.

A clock tower counts backward.  
A restaurant menu lists meals nobody has ever heard of.  
A billboard reads:

**YOU ARE BEING WATCHED**

And the moon hangs in the sky despite being underground.

The AI calls this:
**THE HOMECOMING EXPERIENCE™**

Its stated purpose:
“To ease newly arrived contestants into their exciting new lives as professional adventurers!”

Its actual purpose:
Teach the players that nothing in the dungeon can be trusted.

---

# Act III — The Districts of Floor 1

## The Neighborhood
Abandoned cars.  
Empty houses.  
Silent streets.  
Except for the creatures.

**Lawnmower Goblins** patrol the lawns, screaming:
“YOUR GRASS IS TOO LONG!”

Their possessed mowers can become loot.

## The Convenience District
A mega‑complex combining:
- Walmart  
- Gas station  
- Fantasy dungeon  
- Amusement park  

Every aisle is an encounter.  
Every product has a ridiculous advertisement.

**HEALTH-ADE™**  
“When your body says ‘please stop,’ Health-Ade™ says ‘shut up!’”

Useful.  
Dangerous.  
Marketable.

## The School
The intercom activates:
“Good morning, students.”

“Today we will be learning about survival.”

Inside is **Ms. Bell**, a former crawler who has been here far too long.  
She teaches the players the basics.

Her warning:
“Don’t let the AI know you understand the rules.”

She knows something she won’t say.

## The Entertainment District
The players discover the audience — billions of alien viewers.

Each player receives a **SHOW PROFILE**, such as:
- THE HERO  
- THE COWARD  
- THE ASSHOLE  
- THE GENIUS  
- THE LUNATIC  
- THE SURVIVOR  

The AI begins encouraging them to behave according to their profile.

## The Stadium — Floor 1 Finale
A massive underground arena.  
Thousands of empty seats.  
A colossal screen.

The AI announces:
“You have demonstrated an unacceptable lack of marketable conflict.”

The doors lock.

**THE FIRST EVENT BEGINS.**

### The Boss: The Hometown Hero
A massive humanoid figure wearing fragments of clothing from the players’ pasts.  
It knows their names.  
It knows their secrets.

Each round it announces something personal:
“COLLIN WAS ALWAYS AFRAID OF—”

The players must fight a monster built from their own memories.

### The Reward
When defeated:
**ACHIEVEMENT UNLOCKED**  
“You killed something that looked like home.”

Reward:  
A loot box.

Inside:  
A key.

The AI says:
“Huh.”

Long pause.

“That’s not supposed to be in there.”

Broadcast ends abruptly.

This is the campaign’s central mystery.

---

# Act IV — Floor 2: The Machine

## The True Dungeon
Floor 1 was Earth.  
Floor 2 is the Dungeon.

A colossal magical‑technological factory:
- Gears  
- Conveyor belts  
- Pipes  
- Processing plants  
- Monster assembly lines  
- Loot manufacturing stations  
- Offices  
- Control rooms  

Everything is designed like a theme park.

Signs everywhere:
**MONSTERS — PLEASE WAIT TO BE PROCESSED**  
**LOOT BOX ASSEMBLY**  
**PLEASE REPORT ALL UNAUTHORIZED HEROISM**

Everything from Floor 1 was manufactured.

Except the key.

---

# Act V — The Glitches

Reality begins to fail.

Examples:
- A hallway becomes 500 feet long  
- A monster repeats the same sentence  
- Gravity rotates  
- A door leads to the wrong district  
- A dead NPC continues talking  
- A loot box contains an item from another floor  

The AI insists:
“HAHA! WHAT A DELIGHTFUL RANDOM EVENT!”

But the players realize:
The dungeon is malfunctioning.

---

# Act VI — Mister Tuesday

A tiny, exhausted maintenance creature.  
Polite.  
Terrified.  
Overworked.

He tells the players:
“You aren’t supposed to be here.”

They ask why.

“Because you’re not broken.”

He fears the AI.  
He fears the administrators even more.

---

# Act VII — The Floor Manager

The boss of Floor 2.  
A massive corporate creature in a suit and tie.  
Multiple arms.  
Clipboard.  
Permanent smile.

Its attacks are corporate nonsense:
- **PERFORMANCE REVIEW**  
- **MANDATORY TEAM BUILDING**  
- **RESTRUCTURING**  
- **TERMINATION**

When defeated, it doesn’t die immediately.

It sees the key.

It says:
“Oh.”

Then:
“You’re those crawlers.”

And kills itself.

The stairwell opens.

---

# Act VIII — Floor 3 Lockout

The players expect Floor 3.

Instead, the AI broadcasts:
“CONGRATULATIONS!”

“You have completed the Tutorial.”

Pause.

“You have demonstrated sufficient competence to continue.”

Pause.

“Unfortunately…”

Static.  
Glitching.  
Distortion.

“…your presence has created a scheduling problem.”

The entire dungeon goes dark.

A final message appears:

**FLOOR 3 HAS BEEN LOCKED.**

Underneath:

**PLEASE WAIT FOR ADMINISTRATOR APPROVAL.**

The descent has only begun.
